# Grow a product idea into a proposal, PRD, and prototype

[中文](README.CN.md) · [All examples](../README.md)

> Turn “I want to build…” into documents and screens your team can discuss.

At the beginning of a product idea, the target users, use cases, and boundaries are often unsettled. Writing a PRD immediately can turn assumptions into requirements. Drawing screens too early can put interface details ahead of product decisions.

This demo starts with questions. It clarifies the scope, then connects competitor analysis, product planning, requirements, and prototype previews. Each stage provides something to inspect and revise before moving forward.

**[Watch the product planning demo](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1Mypv6zEUN/)** · 0:55 · Chinese · Published 2026-10-10

## How the idea becomes concrete

| Stage | Question to resolve | Material for discussion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clarification | Who is it for, and which problem comes first? | Product direction and scope |
| Competitors and positioning | What do existing options cover, and where is there room? | Comparison and positioning analysis |
| Proposal and PRD | How should scenarios, functions, and rules fit together? | Readable, editable planning and requirements documents |
| Prototype | How will a user complete the core task? | Screens and flows to present |

## What to look for

Around 0:19, the video shows questions for confirming scope. It then shows several deliverable entries and a document organized around functions, roles, permissions, and recovery behavior. Later segments show intermediate editing and prototype screens.

These outputs give a team something specific to discuss: a boundary to change, a rule to add, or a screen to revise. Product managers, founders, and business teams defining a new requirement can use this as a starting point.

## Try it

Follow the [quick start](../../docs/quick_start.md) and select a product-planning capability in LazyMind. Bring the problem, target users, and known constraints. Use clarification to establish scope, then review the outputs as the task progresses.

The video is a quick tour; leave time for the key product decisions when working on your own task.
