## Scope, method and limitations

- **Claim labels:** `[FACT]` = traceable to the named card and URL; `[INFERENCE]` = analyst judgement derived from the cards; `[UNKNOWN]` = not established by this corpus (also written as "not found" / "not tracked").
- **Citation form:** `[FACT S1 · linear.app/pricing]` — card ID plus original URL. Full URLs are listed in Appendix B.
- **Scope respected:** `request_brief.focus_areas` covers all six default areas (messaging and positioning; product and feature comparison; content and thought leadership; recent announcements; pricing and packaging; market presence). All seven sections are therefore at full depth; none is compressed, and no heading was dropped. Market presence is the one focus area the corpus cannot support — see the `[UNKNOWN]` rows below.
- **Vendor claims are not proof.** Entitlement lists are vendor-listed packaging, not tested behaviour.
- **Silence is not absence.** A capability missing from a sampled page is not evidence the vendor lacks it; S5 explicitly warns against concluding Asana excludes software teams or Linear excludes cross-functional work.
- **Not tracked anywhere in this brief, and never set to zero:** third-party reviews and complaint themes, analyst reports, SEO rankings/traffic/backlinks, social engagement, win-loss and market share, funding/headcount/revenue/company size, negotiated and enterprise pricing, hands-on UX testing, actual AI credit consumption, ROI.
- **Content observations** are a single-date sample of index pages; no frequency, volume, traffic, ranking, sentiment or performance claim is made.
- **Content gaps** are hypotheses within the sampled pages, not established absences across either vendor's estate or the market.
- **Pricing basis:** USD per user per month, billed annually, English pages as of 2026-10-27; taxes, discounts and usage/credit charges excluded; regional quotes differ. Plan features are **not** treated as equivalent across vendors.
- **Boundary:** nothing was sent, purchased, signed into, or drawn from private files; neither product was used or tested.

## 1. Executive Summary

Linear and Asana are converging on AI-assisted work from opposite centres of gravity: Linear presents one system for modern product development spanning roadmap to release, while Asana presents work management at organisational scale in which humans and agents coordinate critical cross-functional workflows around shared plans and goals [FACT S2 · linear.app/features; FACT S5 · asana.com/product]. At 20 seats the entry tiers are nearly level — $2,400.00/year for Linear Basic versus $2,637.60/year for Asana Starter (+$237.60, about +9.9%) — but the second tier diverges sharply, $3,840.00 versus $5,997.60 (+$2,157.60, about +56.2%), and the two ladders are not feature-equivalent [FACT S1 · linear.app/pricing; FACT S4 · asana.com/pricing; INFERENCE]. Both vendors shipped AI capabilities inside the six-month window and both leave AI credit consumption and enterprise pricing unquantified, so the decision turns on workflow scope and cost predictability rather than on sticker price [FACT S3 · linear.app/now; FACT S7 · asana.com/whats-new; FACT S1; FACT S4].

- **Single biggest opportunity** — [INFERENCE] Because entry-tier pricing is within about 10% at 20 seats, the buyer can make this a workflow-fit decision rather than a price decision, and can use the mutual AI-credit/enterprise opacity as leverage: request written overage, credit and enterprise-banding terms from both vendors before any scoring. Neither vendor publishes the numbers a 20-seat budget actually needs [FACT S1 · linear.app/pricing; FACT S4 · asana.com/pricing; UNKNOWN].
- **Single biggest threat** — [FACT S1; FACT S4] Cost creep beyond seat price: AI is metered by credits or request caps with unquantified overage, and both ladders gate capability by tier (Linear gates teams and intelligence; Asana gates goals, portfolios, resource management, approvals/proofing and time tracking to Advanced), so a later need becomes a step change rather than a marginal upgrade.
