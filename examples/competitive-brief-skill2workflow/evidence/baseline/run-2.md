# Competitive brief: Linear versus Asana

**Decision context:** A 20-person software team evaluating product-development coordination and cross-functional launch planning.
**Source/retrieval date:** October 27, 2026.
**Evidence mode:** Offline replay of seven supplied official-source fact cards; no live research or product testing.
**Human review: pending**

[FACT] The cards contain paraphrased vendor statements, not independent validation. **[FACT]** denotes source-backed vendor statements; **[INFERENCE]** denotes analysis or a proposed action; **[UNKNOWN]** denotes information unavailable in the corpus.

[UNKNOWN] Independent reviews, analyst assessments, company size/funding, social engagement, SEO rankings/traffic, market share, win/loss data, negotiated prices, hands-on usability and actual AI usage costs are unavailable. Content gaps below are hypotheses within the sampled pages.

## 1. Executive Summary

[INFERENCE] Linear’s sampled positioning centers on product development from roadmap to release, while Asana’s centers on organizational work management across functions; both deserve consideration for this team’s combined development and launch-planning needs. Their positioning suggests different evaluation priorities, but does not establish superior usability or functional exclusivity. ([S2](https://linear.app/features), [S5](https://asana.com/product))

[INFERENCE] The biggest opportunity is to evaluate one complete workflow—from engineering work through launch approvals—against both products’ stated strengths. The biggest threat is choosing on headline price or AI messaging before checking plan entitlements, rollout and usage charges. ([S1](https://linear.app/pricing), [S4](https://asana.com/pricing), [S7](https://asana.com/whats-new))

## 5. Opportunities

- [INFERENCE] **Anchor the decision in the handoff between development and launch.** Linear’s development emphasis and Asana’s cross-functional emphasis create a useful comparison around the team’s actual coordination boundary. ([S2](https://linear.app/features), [S5](https://asana.com/product))
- [INFERENCE] **Use launch requirements to select the plan.** Determine whether the team needs capabilities explicitly listed in higher tiers—such as Linear Business intake/integrations or Asana Advanced approvals/portfolios—before comparing costs. ([S1](https://linear.app/pricing), [S4](https://asana.com/pricing))
- [INFERENCE] **Evaluate AI through specific tasks.** Event-driven development work and cross-functional assignment/planning are concrete candidate scenarios supported by the announcements. ([S3](https://linear.app/now), [S7](https://asana.com/whats-new))
- [INFERENCE] **Request evidence tailored to a small cross-functional software team.** The sampled messages emphasize development or organizational scale more clearly than the precise combination of 20-person engineering and launch coordination. ([S2](https://linear.app/features), [S5](https://asana.com/product))

## 6. Threats

- [INFERENCE] **Category shortcuts could distort selection.** Assuming Asana cannot serve software teams, or Linear cannot serve cross-functional work, would exceed the evidence. ([S2](https://linear.app/features), [S5](https://asana.com/product))
- [INFERENCE] **Seat pricing could obscure the relevant package.** Different plan entitlements and unquantified AI charges mean the listed subtotals cannot establish equivalent value or total cost. ([S1](https://linear.app/pricing), [S4](https://asana.com/pricing))
- [INFERENCE] **Recent AI announcements could dominate the evaluation prematurely.** Both vendors promote new capabilities, while reliability, adoption and buyer-specific availability remain unverified here. ([S3](https://linear.app/now), [S7](https://asana.com/whats-new))
