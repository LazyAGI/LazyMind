# Competitive brief: Linear versus Asana

**Decision context:** A 20-person software team evaluating product-development coordination and cross-functional launch planning.
**Source/retrieval date:** October 27, 2026.
**Evidence mode:** Offline replay of seven supplied official-source fact cards. No live searches, connectors, or product testing were used.
**Human review: pending.**

[FACT] identifies vendor statements recorded in the cards; these are not independent proof. [INFERENCE] identifies analysis or recommendations. [UNKNOWN] identifies information unavailable in the corpus. Content gaps refer only to the sampled pages.

## 1. Executive Summary

[INFERENCE] Linear presents the more focused product-development narrative, while Asana presents a broader organizational work-management narrative with explicit cross-functional audiences. For this team, the central decision is whether engineering execution or coordination across launch participants should anchor the shared workspace. ([S2](https://linear.app/features), [S5](https://asana.com/product))

[INFERENCE] The biggest opportunity is to evaluate both against one real roadmap-to-launch workflow. The biggest threat is selecting on positioning or seat price alone: plan entitlements, rollout, and unquantified AI charges could change the practical fit and total cost. ([S1](https://linear.app/pricing), [S4](https://asana.com/pricing), [S7](https://asana.com/whats-new))

[FACT] Linear presents itself as a system for modern product development spanning roadmap to release. Its feature emphasis includes projects, initiatives, issue tracking, cycles, AI workflows/agents, analytics, mobile, customer requests, and workplace-request intake through Asks. [INFERENCE] Its implied primary audience is product and engineering teams. The sampled messaging is technical and execution-oriented, organized around planning, building, customer feedback, and AI-assisted development. ## 5. Opportunities

- [INFERENCE] **Anchor the choice in the team’s coordination bottleneck.** Linear’s development emphasis makes it a plausible candidate when roadmap-to-issue execution dominates; Asana’s explicit cross-functional framing makes it a plausible candidate when launch coordination dominates. These are evaluation hypotheses. ([S2](https://linear.app/features), [S4](https://asana.com/pricing), [S5](https://asana.com/product))
- [INFERENCE] **Use one end-to-end launch scenario to resolve the positioning gap.** Connecting a customer request, development work, launch tasks, approvals, and status reporting would expose practical differences that the sampled marketing pages cannot establish. ([S2](https://linear.app/features), [S4](https://asana.com/pricing))
- [INFERENCE] **Compare AI on a concrete task and its allowance.** Event-driven Loops and Asana’s cross-tool AI workflows offer different starting points for testing a recurring coordination task. ([S3](https://linear.app/now), [S4](https://asana.com/pricing), [S7](https://asana.com/whats-new))

## 6. Threats

- [INFERENCE] **Choosing a plan before mapping requirements could understate cost.** Linear separates team limits and Business capabilities; Asana places portfolios and approvals/proofing in Advanced. ([S1](https://linear.app/pricing), [S4](https://asana.com/pricing))
- [INFERENCE] **AI announcements could outrun buyer-specific availability or budget assumptions.** Linear requires credits for certain workflows; Asana specifies allowances and account caps, while rollout requires validation. ([S1](https://linear.app/pricing), [S4](https://asana.com/pricing), [S7](https://asana.com/whats-new))
- [INFERENCE] **Category stereotypes could eliminate a suitable option prematurely.** Asana explicitly addresses developers, and Linear includes workplace-request intake; neither should be excluded solely by its dominant positioning. ([S2](https://linear.app/features), [S5](https://asana.com/product))
- [UNKNOWN] Adoption friction, migration effort, customer sentiment, and real-world reliability remain unresolved because the corpus contains no independent reviews or hands-on testing.
